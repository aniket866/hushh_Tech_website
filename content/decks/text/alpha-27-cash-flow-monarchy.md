## Alpha 27: The Absolute Cash Flow Monarchy

Simplicity is the signature of truth. In an age drowning in factor models, style boxes, and 437 ways to slice beta, we return to first principles: Follow the money. Not the story. Not the multiple. The actual cash generated for owners.

This is the Alpha 27 portfolio—27 names, one rule, zero exceptions. Every dollar allocated in direct proportion to absolute owner earnings: net income for banks and financials, free cash flow for everyone else. No committees. No "strategic tilts." Just the mathematical consequence of asking one question: Who made the most money for shareholders in the last twelve months?

What emerges is not a passive index tracking imaginary "factors" but an active declaration: capital concentrates where profit concentrates. Roughly half in the platforms building the computational and data substrate of the 21st century. A quarter in the institutions moving global money. A quarter in the firms extracting earth's energy. The rest in the essential pipes—healthcare, logistics, connectivity.

For institutional allocators managing billions across sovereign wealth mandates, endowment perpetuity obligations, or multi-generational family office preservation goals, Alpha 27 offers something increasingly rare: a strategy you can explain in one sentence and defend with one formula.

## The One Rule That Governs Everything

### The Formula

\text{Weight}_i = \frac{\text{Owner Earnings}_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{27} \text{Owner Earnings}_j}Where Owner Earnings =

- Net Income for banks, financials, and Berkshire Hathaway
- Free Cash Flow (or its net-income proxy at mega-cap scale) for all other companies

### What This Means in Practice

No price-to-earnings ratios. No momentum scores. No ESG overlays unless they show up in the cash register. This portfolio asks exactly one question of every potential holding: How much actual money did you generate for owners over the trailing twelve months?

The answer to that question—measured in absolute dollars, not relative returns or percentile ranks—determines position size. If Saudi Aramco generated $193 billion in net income and represents 11.7% of the total earnings pool across our 27 candidates, Aramco gets 11.7% of the portfolio. If Apple generated $130 billion and represents 7.9%, Apple gets 7.9%.

This is not "equal weight" naïveté. This is not "market cap weight" index hugging. This is profit weight—the purest expression of letting economic reality, not Wall Street narratives or committee compromise, dictate capital allocation.

For the allocator who has sat through one too many presentations featuring backward-looking correlations dressed up as forward-looking alpha, this approach offers intellectual honesty: we own the world's largest cash-generating machines in proportion to the cash they generate. Everything else is optimization around that spine.

Philosophy in practice: When Microsoft's cloud business accelerates and earnings rise, its weight automatically increases at the next rebalance. When an energy major's margins compress and profits fall, its allocation shrinks. The portfolio is a living mirror of global profit dynamics, not a static bet on last decade's winners.

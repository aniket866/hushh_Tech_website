## The Philosophy

### Why Absolute FCF Growth Matters

## Free Cash Flow: The Ultimate Truth

In a world of accounting gimmicks and adjusted metrics, free cash flow remains the purest measure of business quality. It represents actual cash generated after all investments required to maintain and grow the business.

#### Operating Cash Flow

Cash generated from core business operations after working capital changes

#### Minus Capital Expenditure

Required investments in property, plant, equipment, and technology

#### Equals Free Cash Flow

Discretionary cash available for dividends, buybacks, debt reduction, or reinvestment

Companies that generate large and growing free cash flows possess pricing power, operational excellence, and durable competitive advantages. These are the only businesses worth owning for the long term.

## Why Absolute FCF, Not Relative Metrics

Most investors focus on margins, multiples, or growth rates. We focus on the absolute dollar amount of free cash flow and its growth trajectory. This distinction is critical.

#### The Problem with Relative Metrics

A company with 50% FCF margins generating $100M is less valuable than a company with 20% margins generating $10B. Relative metrics can mislead by focusing on percentages rather than absolute wealth creation.

Similarly, 100% growth from $1M to $2M is less impressive than 10% growth from $50B to $55B — the latter adds 2,750× more absolute cash.

#### Our Absolute FCF Focus

We seek businesses generating tens of billions in current FCF with ability to grow that number by billions annually. NVDA growing FCF by $40B in two years creates more value than 1,000 small companies combined.

This focus on absolute scale plus growth leads us naturally to The27 — the elite aces of aces generating the most cash and growing it fastest.

## The Compounding Power of FCF Growth

When absolute FCF grows consistently, the compounding effect creates extraordinary wealth over time. This is the essence of our long-term strategy.
